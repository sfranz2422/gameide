# Order Up!: picture prompts for Gemini

A cooking rush game. Customers come to the counter with an order bubble; you cook on
grills, fryers and pots, build each order on a plate and serve it before their
patience runs out. Two restaurants to start: a burger shack and a noodle bar.

Every picture will have a stand-in drawn by the build, so the game plays without
any of these. Do them in order and stop whenever you like.

**Saving them:** leave them in `~/Downloads` under whatever name Gemini gives them,
then tell me which are new. I copy them into `showcase/cooking/raw/` straight away,
so they can't be lost.

## How to keep them all in one style

1. **One Gemini chat for everything.** Make picture 1 first, then attach it to every
   later prompt and add: *"In exactly the same art style, outlines, palette and
   lighting as the attached picture."*
2. **No words, numbers, prices or logos** anywhere, not on signs, menus or cups.
   The game writes its own.
3. **Anything marked MAGENTA** goes on flat magenta (#FF00FF), nothing behind it,
   no shadow. The build cuts the magenta away.
4. **Leave clear space between drawings on a sheet**, and keep the rows the prompt
   asks for. Every food the same size as the others of its kind.

## The style (start every prompt with this)

> Detailed 16-bit pixel art for a cheerful cooking game, like a classic Super
> Nintendo game: visible square pixels, clean dark outlines, warm appetising colours,
> glossy food, friendly and bright. No text, no letters, no numbers, no logos, no
> border.

## The restaurants (wide backgrounds, no magenta)

Seen from the customers' side looking at the kitchen counter: the top half is the
restaurant where customers stand, the bottom half is the long empty counter where the
machines and food will go (leave it bare).

**1. The burger shack.**
> A wide (16:9) cosy roadside burger shack interior seen straight on: red and white
> checked tiles, a big window with sunny sky, a few stools, warm lights; the bottom
> half is a long empty steel kitchen counter running the whole width, nothing on it.

**2. The noodle bar.**
> A wide (16:9) cosy noodle bar interior seen straight on, in the same layout: paper
> lanterns, wooden panels, a window with an evening city, plants; the bottom half is
> a long empty wooden kitchen counter running the whole width, nothing on it.
